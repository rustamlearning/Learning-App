Situational Hook

Is AI Helping You Learn, or Doing the Thinking for You?

Di kelas XII, guru memberi tugas menulis paragraf bahasa Inggris tentang “My Future Plan.” Beberapa siswa mencoba menulis sendiri. Mereka membuka buku catatan, mengingat kosakata yang sudah dipelajari, dan mulai membuat kalimat sederhana.

Namun, beberapa siswa lain membuka aplikasi AI di HP. Ada yang menggunakan AI untuk mencari ide, menerjemahkan kalimat, atau memperbaiki grammar. Ada juga yang langsung menyalin jawaban AI tanpa memahami isi tulisannya.

Ketika guru memeriksa tugas, guru menemukan beberapa paragraf terlihat sangat bagus. Tetapi ketika guru bertanya, “What does this sentence mean?” beberapa siswa tidak bisa menjelaskan arti tulisan mereka sendiri.

“Is AI helping you learn, or is it doing the thinking for you?”

Dari situasi ini, siswa belajar bahwa isu besar sering memiliki dua sisi. AI tidak bisa langsung disebut baik atau buruk. Cara penggunaannya menentukan apakah AI menjadi learning tool atau risky shortcut.

MoT — Modelling of the Text

Should Students Use AI for Learning?

IssueArguments ForArguments AgainstRecommendation

Issue

Artificial intelligence, or AI, is becoming part of students’ learning life. Many students now hear about AI from their phones, social media, friends, or online learning tools. Some students use AI to translate words, check grammar, make summaries, find ideas, or ask for practice questions. Because of this, many schools need to discuss an important question: Should students use AI for learning?

This question has two sides. On one hand, AI can be a helpful learning assistant, especially for students who need extra explanation. On the other hand, AI can also become a risky shortcut if students only copy answers without understanding them. Therefore, students, teachers, and schools need to think carefully about how AI should be used.

Arguments For

On one hand, AI can help students understand difficult materials. In English lessons, for example, students may ask AI to explain grammar, give examples of vocabulary, or simplify a reading text. If a student does not understand passive voice, AI can provide more examples and practice sentences. This can support students who need repeated explanation.

Second, AI can help students in remote areas. Students in small islands or remote communities may not always have easy access to many books, private tutors, or large libraries. In places like Sapuka, internet access may become better, but learning resources are still not always equal to those in big cities. In this situation, AI can support students by giving explanations, examples, and practice materials when used wisely.

Third, AI can help students practice writing. Some students are afraid to write English because they worry about grammar mistakes. AI can help them check simple errors, suggest better word choices, or explain why a sentence is incorrect. This does not mean AI should write everything for them. It means AI can help students revise their own writing.

Fourth, AI can make learning more flexible. Students can ask questions outside school hours. They can practice vocabulary at night, review a topic before an exam, or make a simple study plan. This can be helpful for students who must also help their families after school.

Fifth, AI can prepare students for future digital skills. In the future, many jobs may require people to use technology responsibly. Students who learn how to ask good questions, check information, and use AI ethically may become more prepared for college, work, or community projects.

Arguments Against

On the other hand, AI can create serious problems if students use it carelessly. The biggest risk is cheating. Some students may copy a full answer from AI and submit it as their own work. The text may look good, but the student may not understand the meaning. This is not real learning. It only makes the assignment look finished.

Second, AI can give wrong or unclear information. AI may sound confident, but its answer is not always correct. If students believe every answer without checking, they may learn incorrect information. This is dangerous, especially when students use AI for important tasks, sources, or explanations.

Third, students may become too dependent on AI. If students ask AI for every answer, they may stop trying to think by themselves. They may become less confident in writing, reading, solving problems, or making decisions. Learning should train the brain, not replace it.

Fourth, not all students have equal access to AI. Some students may have better phones, stronger internet, or more data packages. Other students may have limited access. If teachers give AI-based tasks without considering this gap, some students may feel left behind. This is called a digital divide.

Fifth, AI use can reduce honesty if there are no clear rules. Students may not know what is allowed and what is not allowed. Is it okay to ask AI for ideas? Is it okay to ask AI to correct grammar? Is it cheating to submit AI-generated paragraphs? Without clear guidance, students may become confused.

Recommendation / Conclusion

In conclusion, students should be allowed to use AI for learning, but only with clear rules, teacher guidance, and honest learning habits. AI should be used as a learning assistant, not as a replacement for student thinking.

A wise rule is: try first, ask second, check again, and write yourself. Students should try to do the task first. After that, they may ask AI for help, compare the answer with teacher explanation, check the information, and rewrite the final work in their own words. If the teacher asks students to mention AI use, students should be honest about it.

AI is not automatically good or bad. It depends on how students use it. Smart students do not only copy. They ask, compare, revise, and understand. Therefore, schools should guide students to use AI wisely, fairly, and responsibly.

A. On One Hand / On the Other Hand

Pattern: On one hand, + positive side. On the other hand, + negative side.

  • On one hand, AI can explain English grammar.
  • On the other hand, students may copy answers without understanding.
  • On one hand, social media can help students communicate.
  • On the other hand, social media can distract students from studying.
B. Some People Argue / Others Believe

Pattern: Some people argue that + sentence. Others believe that + sentence.

  • Some people argue that AI helps students in remote areas.
  • Others believe that AI can make students lazy.
  • Some people argue that social media helps teenagers learn.
  • Others believe that social media can harm students’ focus.
C. Hedging with May / Might / Can / Could

may/might = mungkin. can/could = bisa/dapat.

  • AI may help students learn vocabulary.
  • AI might give incorrect answers.
  • AI can be useful with teacher guidance.
  • AI could be risky without honesty.
D. Recommendation with Should / Need To / Must
  • Students should try first before asking AI.
  • Students need to check AI answers.
  • Students must not copy full answers.
  • Teachers should give clear rules.
  • Schools need to support students with limited internet access.

More Model Texts

Model A — Very Easy: Should Students Use AI to Check Grammar?

Issue. Many students want to write better English sentences. Some students use AI to check grammar mistakes. This creates a question: should students use AI to check grammar?

Arguments For. On one hand, AI can help students find simple grammar mistakes. For example, AI may show that a sentence needs is, are, was, or were. AI can also give examples of correct sentences. This can help students learn from their mistakes. AI can also make students more confident. Some students are afraid to write English because they worry about errors. If AI helps them revise, they may become braver to write.

Arguments Against. On the other hand, AI can be risky if students do not understand the correction. Some students may only copy the corrected sentence without learning why it is correct. AI may also give suggestions that are not suitable for the task. If students always ask AI to fix every sentence, they may not practice enough by themselves.

Recommendation. Students may use AI to check grammar, but they should use it carefully. They should read the correction, ask why it changes, and write the final sentence in their own understanding.

Questions: What can AI help students check? Why can AI make students confident? What is one risk?

Model B — Easy: Should Students Use AI for Homework?

Issue. Many students now use AI when doing homework. Some use it to understand questions, while others use it to copy full answers. This issue has two sides.

Arguments For. On one hand, AI can help students understand difficult homework. If a student does not understand a question, AI can explain it in simpler words. AI can also give examples, vocabulary help, or practice questions. AI may be useful for students who have limited learning resources. Some students cannot always ask a tutor or find extra books. AI can become a learning assistant if students use it honestly.

Arguments Against. On the other hand, AI can make homework dishonest. Some students may copy the full answer and submit it without thinking. The homework may look good, but the student does not really learn. AI can also give wrong answers. If students do not check the answer with books, teachers, or other sources, they may submit incorrect information. Another risk is dependence. Students may stop trying first and ask AI for everything.

Recommendation. Students should be allowed to use AI for homework only with clear rules. They should try first, ask AI for help, check the answer, and rewrite it in their own words. AI should help students learn, not help them cheat.

Model C — Medium: Is Social Media Helping or Harming Teenagers?

Issue. Social media is part of teenage life. Many students use it to communicate, find information, watch videos, and express themselves. However, social media can also create problems. Because of this, people often ask: is social media helping or harming teenagers?

Arguments For. On one hand, social media can help teenagers learn and communicate. Students can watch educational videos, follow school announcements, join class groups, and share useful information. In remote areas, social media may help students see opportunities outside their community. Social media can also help students express creativity. They can make posters, short videos, school campaigns, or English practice content. Moreover, social media can connect students with wider knowledge about scholarships, competitions, environmental issues, and future careers.

Arguments Against. On the other hand, social media can harm teenagers if they use it without control. Some students spend too much time scrolling and forget to study. This can reduce motivation and focus. Social media can also create emotional pressure. Teenagers may compare themselves with others. Online jokes, rude comments, and cyberbullying can hurt students’ confidence. Another problem is misinformation. Students may share news without checking if it is true.

Recommendation. Social media is not always good or bad. The best solution is to use it wisely. Students should follow useful accounts, limit scrolling time, check information before sharing, and use kind words online. Social media should help students grow, not control their life.

Model D — Local Impact: Should Students Care About Climate Change?

Issue. Climate change is often discussed as a global issue. Some students may think it is too far from their daily life. However, students who live in small islands should understand environmental issues because their lives are close to the sea, weather, boats, beaches, and natural resources. The question is: should students care about climate change?

Arguments For. On one hand, students should care about climate change because environmental changes can affect island communities. In many coastal and island areas, people pay attention to stronger weather, high tides, beach erosion, and changing sea conditions. Students in Sapuka also know that the west monsoon, big waves, boat delays, floodwater, and coastal abrasion can affect school, family activities, and transportation. Second, caring about climate helps students understand their own environment. Third, climate awareness can encourage small positive actions such as reducing plastic waste, keeping the school clean, saving electricity, and joining environmental campaigns.

Arguments Against. On the other hand, some people argue that climate change is too big for students to solve. Students may feel that environmental problems need government, experts, and adults. Some students may also feel confused because not every local weather event can be directly blamed on climate change. If the explanation is too difficult, students may lose interest.

Recommendation. Students should care about climate change, but they do not need to feel hopeless. They can start by understanding their local environment and doing simple actions. Teachers should explain climate issues carefully, without making unsupported claims. The best solution is to connect global knowledge with local responsibility.

Model E — Future Choice: Should Young People Leave Their Hometown for Better Opportunities?

Issue. Many young people think about leaving their hometown after finishing school. Some want to continue their study, find work, or gain new experience. For students in small islands, this question can feel very real: should young people leave their hometown for better opportunities?

Arguments For. On one hand, leaving the hometown can give young people more opportunities. They may continue to college, join training, find jobs, or meet people from different places. These experiences can help them grow. Young people may also learn new skills that are not easy to access in their hometown. Later, these skills may help their family and community. Leaving home can also build independence.

Arguments Against. On the other hand, leaving the hometown can be difficult. Young people may feel lonely or far from family. They may face higher living costs, culture shock, or pressure in a new place. Some people also worry that if many young people leave, the hometown may lose its future generation. A small community still needs young people who care, lead, teach, build, and return with new ideas.

Recommendation. Young people may leave their hometown for study or work, but they should not see their hometown as a place to abandon. The best choice is to learn, grow, and keep a strong connection with the community. Some may return. Others may contribute from outside. What matters is not only where they live, but how they use their knowledge responsibly.

Listening Section — Panel Discussion Script

Should We Use AI for Learning?

Moderator: Good morning, everyone. Today, we will discuss an important issue: should students use AI for learning? Some students believe AI is helpful, but others worry that AI can make students lazy. Let’s hear both sides.

Student A: In my opinion, AI can help students learn better. AI can explain grammar, give vocabulary examples, and help students understand difficult reading texts. Some students do not have many books or private tutors, so AI can become a learning assistant.

Student B: I understand that AI can help. However, I worry that some students may copy answers from AI without understanding them. AI can also give wrong information, so students should not believe everything immediately.

Student A: I agree that copying is wrong. But the problem is not AI itself. The problem is how students use it. If students ask AI for examples and then write in their own words, AI can be useful.

Student B: Not all students have the same internet access or devices. If a teacher gives AI homework, some students may have more advantage than others.

Teacher: Both of you give good points. AI can help students, especially with grammar, vocabulary, ideas, and practice. However, students need clear rules. They must not copy full answers. They should try first, ask AI for help, check the answer, and rewrite it in their own words.

Moderator: Thank you. We learned that AI has benefits and risks. A wise student should see both sides before deciding.

Shared Writing Example

Many students now use AI for homework. Some students use it to understand questions, translate words, or check grammar. Other students may use it to copy full answers. Because of this, schools need to discuss whether students should use AI for homework.

On one hand, AI can help students learn. It can explain difficult questions in simpler words. For example, if students do not understand passive voice, AI can give examples and practice sentences.

On the other hand, AI can be risky. Some students may copy a full answer and submit it as their own work. This is not honest learning. AI can also give wrong information, so students should not believe every answer without checking.

The best solution is to allow AI with clear rules. Students should try first, ask AI for help, check the answer, and rewrite it in their own words. AI should be a helper, not a replacement for thinking.
