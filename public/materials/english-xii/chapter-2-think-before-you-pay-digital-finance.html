MoT — Modelling of the Text

Should Sapuka Students Use E-Money?

Thesis

Sapuka students can use e-money, but they should use it carefully and responsibly. Digital payment is becoming more common because internet access is easier than before. E-money can be useful, but it also has risks. Because of that, students need financial literacy, self-control, and guidance from parents and teachers.

Arguments

First, e-money can make transactions faster and more practical when internet access is available. For example, a student may need to buy a data package for online learning.

Second, e-money can help students learn digital financial literacy. They can check balance, read receipts, record spending, and understand transaction history.

Third, responsible use of e-money can teach budgeting and self-control. Students can learn to separate needs from wants.

Fourth, digital payment can help families send money for school needs, transportation, or emergencies. However, students still need cash because not every transaction in Sapuka can be done digitally.

Counterargument

Some people argue that students should not use e-money because it can make them spend too much money. Some students may top up games too often, buy unnecessary items, or follow online promotions without thinking carefully. Digital scams, fake links, wrong transfers, and unstable internet can also create problems.

Rebuttal

These risks are real, but the solution is not to reject e-money completely. Students need guidance, rules, and spending limits. They must check links, receiver numbers, and trusted sources before transferring money.

Conclusion

In conclusion, e-money can be useful for Sapuka students if it is used wisely. Students must think before paying, protect their accounts, avoid scams, and balance digital money with cash.

More Model Texts

Thesis: Students should think before they top up online games. Top up can be fun, but it can also make students spend too much money.

Arguments: Students have limited pocket money. Digital payment feels fast and easy. Some top up offers can be risky.

Counterargument: Some students say that top up is okay because it is entertainment.

Rebuttal: Entertainment is okay, but students still need limits.

Conclusion: Needs should come first. Wants should come later.

Cash is still important in Sapuka, even though digital payment is becoming more common. Not all daily transactions can be done digitally. Internet signal can be unstable. Some people believe digital payment is faster, but Sapuka students still need cash for daily needs and emergencies.

Digital payment can be useful in a small island community like Sapuka, but students should not depend on it completely. It can help families send money quickly and help students buy data packages. On the other hand, signal can be unstable and some shops accept only cash. Therefore, students should use both cash and e-money wisely.

ResponseHowever, students can use e-money safely if...
ClosingIn conclusion, e-money can be useful, but students must...

Sample Mini Debate

Pro Team: We believe students can use e-money because it is useful and practical. For example, students can buy data packages and receive money from family more easily.

Contra Team: We disagree because e-money can be risky. Students may spend too much money on games or online shopping.

Pro Team: That risk is real. However, students can use e-money safely if they check links, set spending limits, and ask parents before big purchases.
