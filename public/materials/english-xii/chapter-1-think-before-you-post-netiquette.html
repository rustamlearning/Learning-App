2. Situational Hook: A Small Post, A Big Impact

Suatu sore di Sapuka, beberapa siswa baru pulang sekolah. Jalan dekat sekolah masih tergenang air karena banjir rob. Seorang siswa melepas sepatunya agar tidak basah. Temannya mengambil foto dan mengirimkannya ke grup WhatsApp kelas. Beberapa siswa mengirim emoji tertawa dan komentar bercanda.

Awalnya, mereka menganggap itu lucu. Namun, siswa yang ada di foto merasa malu karena foto itu tersebar tanpa izin. Keesokan harinya, ia menjadi pendiam di kelas. Guru kemudian mengajak siswa berdiskusi: “Apakah bercanda online selalu aman? Apakah kita boleh memposting foto teman tanpa izin? Bagaimana cara membuat grup kelas menjadi tempat yang aman dan saling menghargai?”

Key idea: Di pulau kecil seperti Sapuka, dunia online dan dunia nyata sangat dekat. Komentar di HP bisa memengaruhi hubungan di sekolah, keluarga, dan masyarakat.

6. MoT — Modelling of the Text

Main Model Text

Think Before You Post: Netiquette for Sapuka Students

Text type: Hortatory Exposition • Structure: Thesis → Arguments → Recommendation

Thesis

Sapuka students should practice netiquette every day because online behavior affects real relationships in a small island community. In Sapuka, students meet their friends not only at school, but also near their homes, at the mosque, at the harbor, on the beach, or in community activities. Because many people know each other, one post, one photo, one screenshot, or one comment can spread quickly and affect real life.

Argument 1

First, students should think before posting because one photo can have a big impact. A photo that looks funny to one person may be embarrassing for another person. For example, a student may post a picture of a friend walking through floodwater after school. Some classmates may laugh in the class WhatsApp group. However, the friend in the photo may feel ashamed because the picture is shared without permission. In a small island community, online jokes do not stay only online. Students meet again at school and in the community, so online words can hurt real feelings.

Argument 2

Second, students must respect privacy. Sharing photos, videos, or screenshots without permission can break trust. A private chat should stay private. A photo of a friend should not be shared if the friend does not agree. When students respect privacy, they show maturity and responsibility. Respecting privacy also helps keep friendship safe.

Argument 3

Third, class WhatsApp groups should become learning spaces, not places to embarrass others. A class group can help students receive homework, ask questions, remind friends about assignments, and get school information. It can also help students when the internet connection is limited or when students cannot come to school because of bad weather or boat delays. However, if the group is full of mocking, harmful jokes, or unclear information, students may feel uncomfortable and stop using it for learning.

Argument 4

Fourth, Sapuka students need to check information before forwarding it. In island life, information about boat schedules, weather, exams, scholarships, or school activities is important. False information can confuse students and families. Before forwarding a message, students should ask: Who sent this? Is the source clear? Has the teacher confirmed it? Is the information still valid? Checking information is a simple action, but it can protect many people from misunderstanding.

Argument 5

Fifth, a good digital footprint can protect students’ future. What students post today may still be seen later. Harmful comments, screenshots, or rude words can damage trust. On the other hand, positive posts, respectful comments, and helpful messages can show good character. Digital behavior is part of personal reputation. Students who use the internet wisely show that they are ready to become responsible young adults.

Recommendation

Therefore, Sapuka students should practice netiquette every day. Think before you post. Ask permission before sharing photos or videos. Do not share private chats. Use kind words in class groups. Check information before forwarding it. Stop online bullying. Make class WhatsApp groups safe learning spaces. In a small island community, respect matters online and offline. Be smart, be kind, and be responsible.

12. Listening Section — Audio Script

A Problem in the Class Group

Characters: Sari, Rian, and Ms. Lina.

Ms. Lina: Good morning, everyone. Today, we need to talk about what happened in our class WhatsApp group yesterday.

Rian: Ma’am, is this about the photo?

Ms. Lina: Yes, Rian. I want us to learn from it. Sari, would you like to say something?

Sari: I felt embarrassed, Ma’am. I was walking through the floodwater near school, and I took off my shoes because I did not want them to get wet. Then my photo was sent to the class group.

Rian: I am sorry, Sari. I thought it was only a joke. I did not know you felt embarrassed.

Sari: I know you were joking, but many friends sent laughing emojis. I felt uncomfortable.

Ms. Lina: Thank you for explaining, Sari. A joke online can hurt real feelings. In a small island community like Sapuka, students meet again at school, at home, at the mosque, or near the harbor. Online actions can affect offline relationships.

Rian: I understand now, Ma’am. I should have asked permission first.

Ms. Lina: That is right. Before sharing a photo, we should ask permission. We must respect privacy. We should not share private chats, embarrassing photos, or screenshots without permission.

Sari: I also think our class group should be used for learning and reminders, not for making someone feel ashamed.

Ms. Lina: Exactly. Class groups can help us share homework, school announcements, weather information, or boat schedule updates. But we must check information before forwarding it.

Rian: Sari, I am sorry. I will delete the photo and tell our friends not to share it again.

Sari: Thank you, Rian. I appreciate that.

Ms. Lina: Good. Remember, students can learn and change. Let us make our class group safe, respectful, and useful for learning.

Shared Writing Example

Students Should Make Class Groups Respectful Learning Spaces

Students should make class WhatsApp groups respectful learning spaces. A class group is not only a place for chatting. It is also a place to receive homework, school announcements, reminders, and important information.

First, class groups help students learn. Teachers can send assignments, and students can ask questions about lessons. When students use the group well, everyone can get information faster.

Second, harmful comments can hurt friends’ feelings. In Sapuka, students meet again at school and in the community. A joke in the group can become a real problem if it makes someone feel ashamed. Because of that, students should use kind words.

Third, students need to check information before forwarding it. News about boat schedules, weather, exams, or scholarships should come from a clear source. False information can confuse students and families.

Therefore, students should use class groups wisely. Ask permission before sharing photos. Do not share private chats. Check information before forwarding it. Let us make our class group safe, respectful, and useful for learning.
